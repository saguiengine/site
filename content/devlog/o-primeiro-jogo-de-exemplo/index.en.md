*A short note from stage 2, about delivery 10. The times are provisional,
measured on my PC.*

Until now, the test game was a square on a plain background. Delivery 10
brings **Meu Jogo** ("My Game", in Portuguese), the first example of a
platform game (a *platformer*): a level three screens wide, in *pixel art*
(drawing made of visible little squares). It is what the engine runs when
nobody picks another project.

## What is in the level

- The player is the little monkey from the drawings on the site and in the
  editor. It walks with the arrow keys, the W, A, S and D keys or the
  controller. For now it only walks, in any direction, even up, through
  the air; the jump only plays its sound. Gravity and the real jump come
  in stage 3.
- The floor, the plank platforms and the clouds come from **Mata**
  ("woods", in Portuguese), the *tileset* (the set of 16 × 16 pixel
  squares, the *tiles*, that the scenery is built from) that already showed
  in the editor's drawings; the sign is Mata's, redrawn bigger to fit the
  phrase. Bushes and flowers, also from Mata, decorate the floor.
- The clouds are copies of a mold, the model piece that repeats across the
  level (the *prefab*); one of them comes from a bigger variant of the
  mold.
- The background has four layers: the sky in bands, snowy mountains,
  hills, and mounds with trees. A program draws this background (it is a
  *procedural* background, generated by code) and can also make the night
  version, which shows in the editor's drawings.

## The camera and the sign

The **Camera**, which arrived in delivery 7, now follows the player in a
real level and stops at the edges, without showing the void beyond them.

The sign says "Use as setas para andar" in Portuguese and "Use the arrow
keys to walk" in English, in the language of whoever plays. It is the
first time the game uses a font kept in the project itself: monogram, in a
grid of 6 × 12 pixel letters.

## The same game everywhere

A 60-second *replay* (the recording of the commands, which the game
repeats by itself) goes to the end of the level and back. It ends
with the same *hash*, a fingerprint of the game's state that changes if
anything changes, on Windows, on Linux, on macOS and in *WebAssembly*
(*wasm*), the program format that browsers use; for now, this test runs
outside the browser. Saving halfway and going on in another run also gives
the same result.

On my PC, the game opens and shows the first frame (each picture that
shows on the screen) in 0.6 s (the official limit is 1.5 s), and 99% of the
frames take less than 3 ms (a ms is a thousandth of a second).

## What the tester and I found

The tester, the checker who took no part in the work, saw that the sign's
letters came out broken: the text was at a size other than the one the
pixel art font was drawn for, and pixels were missing. Now the sign uses
the project's font at the right size, and a test compares the sign pixel
by pixel. And, playing, I saw that the art was not the one in the editor's
drawings: it was swapped for Mata, and the background got the pixel art it
has now.

## What comes now

Delivery 11 tidies up the tests and the continuous integration (the *CI*,
the robot that runs the tests on every change). Then comes the review at
the end of stage 2.
